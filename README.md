# Night Neighbourhood

A small browser game for 2–6 university friends who already know each other.
Everyone owns a bedroom in a shared house. Friends can walk, sit at a study
table, visit an open room, talk, and keep a question and their own next step.

## What good means here

When someone is stuck, they should find a familiar peer who has voluntarily
chosen **Can chat**, have a short conversation, and keep an author-written next
step. **Quiet** is equally valid. Nobody must decorate, post a question, reply
or run a timer before belonging here. This is a design hypothesis: demand,
comfort and usefulness have not yet been established with target users.

Ownership should be recognisable, presence truthful, and saved work recoverable.
An avatar represents an actual connected resident; being online or sitting does
not prove attention or study. Willingness, location and bedroom permission are
separate. The conversation corner remains shared with everyone in that room.

Privacy, ownership, capacity, conflicts and committed effects can be checked.
Warmth, ease, valuable conversations and voluntary return need people to judge
them. Automated sessions and screenshots cannot establish those qualities.
We must earn the extra cost of moving through a place: a fair alternative is a
private Discord group with agreed availability, question threads and summaries.

## Enter and make it yours

Create a house for 2–6 and share its eight-character code with familiar friends.
Offline residents keep their bedrooms. Keep this browser's cookie to return;
a private recovery key restores the same identity and replaces old sessions.
The invite code admits a new member and cannot recover another person's room.

Use WASD, arrows or the visible movement controls. Click a door, seat or board
to approach it; **E** interacts nearby and stands you up. **Overview** reveals
the whole room; **Recenter** returns to close avatar-following play. Typing
suppresses walking. Choose Quiet or Can chat explicitly.

Send text at the bottom and open **Chat** for the transcript. Rooms retain the
latest 100 messages for up to seven days. Quiet hides floating previews while
the transcript remains available. Joining includes retained lounge history;
opening a bedroom shares its saved arrangement and retained history with current
housemates. Closing it returns visitors to the lounge.

In your room, choose a palette and arrange up to ten pieces from six furniture
categories. Preview before saving; the server checks collision and arrival paths.
The board has one current card per resident and twelve inactive cards. Authors
control their goal, question, resource, next step and closure. Saving a next step
does not declare a question solved. Departures remain unfinished and preserve
owned rooms/cards in private exports. Owners can transfer, remove and reinstate;
removal rotates the code. Transfer before leaving with others; the last resident
can archive the house.

## Influences and evidence

[Kind Words 2](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2118120/Kind_Words_2_lofi_city_pop/)
informs warm text exchange; [gogh](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3213850/announcements/)
shows existing avatar, decoration and co-study ideas.
[Robin Sloan](https://www.robinsloan.com/notes/home-cooked-app/) informs our small,
familiar audience. We use original procedural house/avatar models; the preserved
`/legacy/` rehearsal credits [KayKit Furniture Bits](https://kaylousberg.itch.io/furniture-bits).
Timers, uploads, public discovery and large building catalogues remain extensions.

Node, SQLite, Three.js and Socket.IO share one process. Durable actions commit
before acknowledgement; motion and seat leases remain transient. The local
candidate, checks, research and limitations are in [validation](docs/implementation/VALIDATION.md)
and [PLAN](PLAN.md). Published Crit8 remains frozen until an authorised release.
AI assisted this factual draft and implementation; the student reviews the final
argument and process claims.
