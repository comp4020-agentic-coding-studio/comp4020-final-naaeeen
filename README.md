# Night Neighbourhood

A visitor can leave a blue pane in a shared lamp, write that it is for the quiet after rain, and return to find it beside somebody else's colour. Night Neighbourhood is a small browser space built around that interaction. You can choose a name and window colour, arrange your room, and contribute one changeable pane to the courtyard lamp. I wanted a quiet social sandbox with room for personal expression and future updates, without turning every visit into a task to complete.

## What would make this good?

A good neighbourhood would let people recognise something they made, understand what another person added, and come back without losing their place. The lamp gives separate contributions one shared form: each author controls their own pane while seeing it alongside others. Arranging a room makes returning personal, but shared authorship is the reason this needs more than one visitor. Watching another pane arrive should matter because someone chose it, not because a counter increased.

Quiet participation matters too. There are no likes, rankings, streaks or compulsory replies. Viewing publishes nothing. A saved window represents a contribution left here, not proof that its author is online. I want the space to tolerate silence, rather than treating an unanswered light as a failure.

## Where the idea comes from

[gogh's official updates](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3213850/announcements/) show that multiplayer rooms, custom objects, shared focus timers and chat already exist. Those features alone cannot make this project original. [Kind Words 2](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2118120/Kind_Words_2_lofi_city_pop/) places several forms of gentle exchange in a town and avoids followers and likes. I take its warmth as a reference, not a claim about copying its engine or visual technique.

[Robin Sloan's home-cooked app](https://www.robinsloan.com/notes/home-cooked-app/) makes a case for software valuable to a few particular people. That supports my longer-term aim of a small friends' circle. [LumiTouch](https://tangible.media.mit.edu/project/lumitouch/) explores light as emotional communication; its [paper](https://alumni.media.mit.edu/~benres/verbiage/chi2001-ac.pdf) also describes anxiety when a response is expected. This makes voluntary contribution and withdrawal design questions, not decoration. These sources suggest a direction; they do not establish this app's social effects or novelty.

[Tiny Glade](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2198150/Tiny_Glade/) and gogh also informed the scene-firstHUD: compact tools let the world lead. This is an interface choice, not free walking or an economy.

## What this first version offers

This C8 version is a **public rehearsal**. No email or account is needed. Saving shares your name and window colour; saving a pane shares its name, colour and optional note with other visitors. Keep this browser's cookies to return to your room. Clearing them or changing browsers creates another visitor; recovery is not available. An owned room is not a promise of confidentiality.

The room has six movable pieces across four types: two chairs, a table, a lamp and two plants. Native controls move and turn them. The scene combines [KayKit Furniture Bits](https://kaylousberg.itch.io/furniture-bits) models, credited under CC0, with procedural architecture and plants. Private circles, uploads, a large catalogue and room creation are later possibilities.

Ownership, collision rejection, save/retry, withdrawal and restart persistence have local automated checks. Two Chromium contexts have exercised shared changes, retained-cookie return and keyboard controls at desktop and phone-sized viewports. Deployment, physical phones and human trials are still unverified. Whether people understand the lamp, assign meaning to it or feel pressure to respond needs their judgement. [The validation record](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-naaeeen/blob/main/docs/C8-VALIDATION.md) keeps those boundaries visible.
