# Crit 9 reflection

My breakthrough for this crit was choosing quiet company as the focus. I pushed back on the suggestion to centre this crit on preserving
a draft after a delayed reply. That matters, but it sounded more like repairing
a bug than explaining why this house should work in a particular way.

The question I chose was how friends can share a space without being expected
to interact all the time. A connected avatar gives us presence, but it cannot
tell us whether its person welcomes a conversation. I accepted Quiet and Can chat
as a way to express that difference, separately from room access. There is a cost:
people must maintain the state, and a friend can still mistake it for a promise.
An invitation would make agreement clearer, but make a short exchange slower.

The deeper comparisons I kept asking for set a standard for the developer I want
to be: someone who can explain the capability a design gives up. The agent helped research
alternatives and inspect the implementation; I redirected the discussion when
it focused too heavily on repair. I also want to keep the distinction between
what tests establish and what people judge. Tests can protect the Quiet default
and message behavior. They cannot tell me whether friends feel comfortable using
the convention. That is the next question I would put to users.
