# Crit 8 reflection

*AI-assisted draft from my stated aims and the repository's recorded work, 6 October 2026. This needs my personal review before submission; it does not claim a completed deployment or human trial.*

The breakthrough was reducing the idea to a complete saved interaction. I wanted a cosy project with personal rooms, meaningful DIY and room to grow. That could easily become a long list of editors and social features. The C8 requirement gave it a sharper test: can a stranger make something and find it again? A window and one owned pane in the shared lamp answer that question while retaining the larger idea.

This also meant changing the original invite-only plan. Requiring an invitation before a marker could contribute was wrong for this checkpoint. The public rehearsal needs an explicit visibility notice, rather than borrowing the privacy promise of the future friends' circle. That change matters more than adding another piece of furniture.

The developer I want to become can direct agents without treating their confidence as evidence. Reusing Assignment 2's research and review methods is useful only if I check the new requirements, runtime and behaviour myself. Here, real SQLite tests and two browser contexts give technical evidence, but they cannot tell me whether the lamp feels meaningful or creates pressure to reply. I want to keep those questions open for people, and be willing to change the direction after their use. A good outcome should be something worth returning to, not simply a large feature list that passes tests.
