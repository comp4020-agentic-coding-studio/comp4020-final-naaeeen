# Crit 10 reflection

I began with a house where friends could move, decorate and study together.
Auditing its permissions made me look at the shared board differently. If I add
an idea and a friend changes it, which version should I be able to keep when I
leave?

My breakthrough was seeing that permission to collaborate leaves this question
unanswered. [Caspi and Blau's work on psychological ownership](https://www.openu.ac.il/Lists/MediaServer_Documents/PersonalSites/AvnerCaspi/Caspi_Blau2011.pdf)
helped me think about the difference between contributing something and feeling
that the resulting work is mine. A technically shared object can carry different
expectations for its contributors.

Our board keeps the current shared version alongside each person's latest
submitted versions. An existing test makes the choice concrete: a guest places
an object at x=12, the host changes it to x=25, then removes the guest. The guest
loses access to the shared board but can still export their x=12 submission.
Removal governs future participation without making that saved submission
conditional on the host's approval.

There is a cost. The export can be outdated and incomplete. Giving the guest the
whole current board would preserve context but also disclose peers' later work.
Their submitted version may itself contain earlier contributions from friends;
it records an action, rather than establishing sole authorship.

I want to become a developer who follows a reassuring promise through an awkward
case, and makes the compromise understandable to the person living with it.

AI assisted with drafting and editing. This reflection draws on the project's
actual implementation and design tradeoffs.
