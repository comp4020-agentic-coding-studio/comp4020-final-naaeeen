# Writing research used for the PROCESS comparison

The user reported that the 510-word draft was too abstract and asked for concrete
agent practices, multiple A/B comparisons and more natural writing. The factual
claims and rubric were fixed before candidates were requested.

Sources checked in this pass:

- [A2 brief](https://comp.anu.edu.au/courses/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/assessments/assignment-2/): keep one narrative connecting the course-design position, encoded rules/checks and human judgement; explain why a choice was made and how it was accepted. The 400–600-word guidance supports selection rather than a complete activity list.
- [Australian Government plain-language guide](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/style-manual-resources/quick-guides/quick-guide-plain-language): lead with what the reader needs, prefer familiar words and active sentences, keep one main idea per paragraph. We apply these to a tutor reading quickly; its sentence-length advice is not used as a mechanical prose score.
- [GOV.UK clear-language guidance](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/): explain specialised terms through the actual action, and use plain English for specialist readers too. The revision should explain the broken link check before naming the parser or evaluator.

The initially tried long Australian Style Manual URL was inaccessible. The
quick-guide URL above was read successfully. The older GOV.UK writing/tone route
redirected without exposing guidance; only the clear-language page above informs
these findings. No inaccessible page is counted as read.

Application: replace 'bounded delegation' with which agent owned which files;
replace 'mechanical acceptance' with what the test actually accepted; replace
'documents preserve decisions' with what a returning agent can read to find its
next task. Keep the before/after and reasons in the narrative. Use no AI detector,
forbidden-word tally, fabricated reader trial or promised grade.

The editorial protocol and its criteria are in protocol.json. Two independent
candidate writers receive the same evidence packet. Separate anonymous-label
reviewers compare the drafts; the parent checks their findings and constructs a
second candidate for a further comparison. Their preferences are model editorial
judgements. The student remains the actual reader and author approving the account.

An additional primary source was read for this writing pass: Anthropic's
[discernment report](https://academy.claude.com/tutorials/discernment-toolkit), dated
10 September2026. Its interviews describe checking AI output against sources and
using work-specific review questions, including audience and defensible claims.
We apply those practices here through the fixed evidence packet and targeted
editorial rubric. The self-selected interview sample does not establish that our
chosen workflow improves all writing; no suggested installation or external
contact workflow from the page was adopted. This source informed the present
writing review, not the earlier implementation retroactively.

The parent also inspected the current [Claude prompting guidance](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices), specifically its response-format and prompt-chaining sections. It suggests positively specifying the desired prose and separating drafting, criteria-based review and revision when intermediate outputs need inspection. These are provider recommendations to test in this task, not evidence that the same prompt guarantees good writing in Codex. No model-specific settings were imported.
