# Requirements and capability audit

Maintain one traceability matrix with: requirement ID, original source, mandatory
or chosen status, actors, user-visible promise, relevant data/state transition,
verification method, current result and unresolved question.

Refresh the live course contract at scope changes and delivery. Preserve exact
meanings of multi-user, real-time, persistence, deployment and written evidence.
Distinguish the initial two-week build from the official deadline and future
development. Check both full marking viewports against promised core behaviour.

Turn the product intent into actors and surfaces, membership/ownership, lifecycle
states, transitions, input/output contracts, failure and recovery, persistence,
observability and release boundaries. Separate policy from architectural preference.
Write capability boundaries before splitting implementation among agents.

For Night Neighbourhood consider private and shared space, voluntary presence,
room editing, gifts/messages, collaborative activity and revisiting artifacts.
Explicitly distinguish durable state from transient connection or movement state.
Do not interpret every desirable feature as a release requirement.

Native submission checks can verify files and executable properties; they cannot
establish factual truth, source interpretation, visual appeal or social benefit.
Keep those acceptance questions and their actual evidence explicit.

Inherited audit examples live under `../reference/a2/docs/planning/`; their course
contracts, deadlines and PASS states are historical and must be replaced.
