# ADR 0003: world-first game presentation

6 October 2026. The owner rejected the initial presentation as too webpage-like
and requested game-like UI/UX. This is actual owner feedback, not a fabricated
participant result. Preserve the saved interaction; redesign the presentation.

The parent visually inspected specific official Steam screenshots through the
native browser. No copyrighted images/assets were downloaded into the repository.
Kind Words 2 fills the frame with its world and places a dialogue bubble near
characters (ss_9204e2345f1fdbb9d243fd76cfa49e59f9ade5ca).

https://store.steampowered.com/app/2198150/Tiny_Glade/
These observations do not establish every interaction in those games.

A was large heading + boxed scene + form/sidebar. B makes the world the canvas:
compact HUD, contextual window/lamp card, edit toolbelt, neighbourhood window dock
and quiet confirmation. Select B to address the explicit owner preference. No
statistical A/B or universal aesthetic result is claimed; borrow principles,
not art. Native labelled/keyboard/touch controls remain required. Game presentation
does not justify hidden save/error recovery, fake online people or clipped mobile
forms. On phone, use a meaningful world area and compact scrollable action sheet.
No walking, scoring, economy or quests are implied for Crit8.

Compare A at38358b3 and B with matched room/API/tasks and both course viewports.
Presentation includes the available canvas area, not just type/colour. Record any
unmatched data/views; model inspection is not user preference. Stop after core
actions and material findings are resolved, rather than expanding features.

HUD panels remain clients of the same semantic commands. Later catalogues,
visited rooms, avatar options and private circles reuse these boundaries.
Free walking/high-frequency movement require a separate decision and evidence.
