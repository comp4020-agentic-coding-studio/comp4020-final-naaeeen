# Design research: participation, place and attention

Checked 12 October 2026. Primary sources inform design hypotheses, not human
validation or novelty. Parent read decisive sections and verified current product
docs; researcher read additional full relevant sections. No external product trial.

## Social information can create both coordination and pressure

Erickson and Kellogg, TOCHI 7(1), March 2000,
[Social Translucence](https://smg.media.mit.edu/library/erickson2000.pdf),
DOI 10.1145/344949.345004; §§2.1–2.3,4.2,5.1. The paper connects visibility,
awareness and accountability, while stressing privacy tension. Babble deployment
experience included implementer use and acquainted groups, not our student users.
Making activity visible can support coordination and also peer pressure.
Inference: chosen approachability is useful social information, but seeing it
does not establish attention, response obligation or enforced consent. Quiet still
discloses metadata; any reduction in reply pressure remains untested.

## A geometric house does not automatically become a social place

Harrison and Dourish, CSCW 1996,
[Re-Place-ing Space](https://dourish.com/~dourishc/publications/1996/cscw96-place.pdf),
DOI 10.1145/240080.240193; §§3–5,8.2,9–10. Shared conventions and appropriation
give settings meaning; geometry supplies opportunities. Their virtual-door example
warns that a hard access mechanism can erase socially negotiated meanings.
Inference: our door is an explicit access rule, not a universal real-world signal
of busyness. Separate willingness reduces that conflation but adds complexity.
DIY and persistent shared artifacts may support appropriation; they do not prove
warmth or long-term belonging. This is interpretive CSCW research, not a controlled
experiment validating our rooms.

## Lightweight participation needs low-cost transitions

Sun and Greenberg, GROUP 2010,
[Places for Lightweight Group Meetings](https://grouplab.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/grouplab/uploads/Publications/Publications/2010-ComeTogether.Group.pdf),
DOI 10.1145/1880071.1880111; §§2,5–6. They address graded involvement,
persistent artifacts and easy transitions into interaction. Their own evaluation
used 12 HCI-informed walkthrough participants, no field deployment; missing VoIP
was a limitation. Prior Community Bar findings described room/invitation friction
and one-room crowding. Inference: quiet participation and accessible shared tools
are plausible, but neither text sufficiency nor our soft status is established.

## Peripheral participation can be deliberately adjustable

Weiser and Brown, 1996 author essay,
[The Coming Age of Calm Technology](https://www.johnseelybrown.com/calmtech),
periphery/examples; [author retrospective](https://www.johnseelybrown.com/calmtech.pdf)
corroborates the original date. Information can move between peripheral and focal
attention under individual control. Mere proximity may demand attention socially.
Inference: suppress previews while leaving retrievable conversation/unread cues.
This is a conceptual argument, not evidence that warm colors, music or our
notification choices calm people or improve study performance.

## Counterevidence to manual availability

Fogarty, Lai and Christensen, 2004,
[Presence versus Availability](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jfogarty/publications/ijhcs2004.pdf),
DOI 10.1016/j.ijhcs.2003.12.016. The workplace deployment found context often
interpreted as presence; authors also criticize manual DND staleness. Their study
does not prove our declaration policy superior. Keeping a visible last-set time
and resetting a new lease is partial mitigation, not attention detection. An
invitation may provide stronger current agreement at higher interaction cost.

## What the primary literature does and does not let us say

A defensible design chooses between visibility, voluntary engagement, privacy,
manual maintenance and spontaneity. It explains which capability is sacrificed
and when another policy would be preferable. A statement that 3D avatars or a
status toggle necessarily reduce loneliness, interruptions or improve grades
would exceed every source and our current evidence.

Configured Discord remains a fair whole-product comparison. If known friends
already express willingness and retain useful exchanges there, the extra house
movement/setup needs a separate experiential reason. That future judgement cannot
be replaced by source citations or model-review agreement.
