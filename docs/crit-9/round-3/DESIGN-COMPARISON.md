## Common-task scenario comparison for S

Analytical design walkthrough, NOT RUN as a user experiment or alternative
software prototype. Fixed context: two-to-six known friends, individual work,
brief occasional conversation, same room/avatars, optional ordinary company.

| Common situation | Online/open convention | Activity inference | Explicit soft cue | Invitation-first |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Join for company while concentrating | Lowest setup, weak way to distinguish desired engagement | May infer busy, but idle/quiet studying ambiguous | Quiet expresses preference with upkeep | Can decline, with request interruption and extra steps |
| Idle-looking friend welcomes brief help | Easy approach but no chosen indication | Same pose may represent different intention | Can chat carries chosen willingness, not attention proof | Acceptance establishes current engagement more clearly |
| Open room for visiting while wanting quiet | Access may be misread as availability | Location can become a misleading proxy | Separate access and willingness | More explicit negotiation but visiting/chat scopes need design |
| Forget an earlier declaration | No declaration to maintain | Automatic cues update, still uncertain intent | Staleness remains; chosen time/default helps but does not solve | Repeated invitations are current, at interaction cost |

The same observed idle avatar can represent willing or unwilling participation.
That is a mechanism-level reason for declaration, not a measured error rate.
A group preferring spontaneous hangout may reasonably choose the first policy.
Strict anti-interruption requirements may favor the last. A separate public
availability and private preview preference is also a functional alternative:
more expressive, but with additional controls and state to interpret.

## S versus strongest owner-led alternate W

W answers a different concrete choice: retain avatars/world/room conversation
during brief notes, or switch into a complete shared canvas with companion chat
for sustained work. Current board mode suspends house interaction and uses its own
house-wide conversation/presence. Both standalone and house access work; continuous
spatial immersion is sacrificed. It is not seamless room-chat continuity.

A small in-world board can be better for brief annotations; an always-web board
can be better for primarily joint work. Current dual mode buys full tools at
context-switching and duplicate-entry/control cost. Its architecture comparisons
and native tasks support implementation, not a human preference experiment.
