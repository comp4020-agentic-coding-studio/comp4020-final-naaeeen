# Crit 9 discussion notes

## A short explanation

A friend can sit in our lounge while concentrating on a paragraph. Seeing their
avatar gives company, but it does not tell me they welcome a conversation.
I chose a soft Quiet/Can chat cue separately from location and door access.

The alternatives offer useful things. An open hangout has fewer controls and more
spontaneous encounters. An invitation makes agreement clearer but slows a brief
exchange. Our familiar-friend setting accepts manual upkeep and stale states in
return for a low-cost declaration. Quiet still receives messages; it is not DND.

## Demo

Use two independent browsers. Join the same house, show both connected, and
change Quiet to Can chat without moving or opening a door. Show the other browser
updating without reload. Send a short message to a Quiet resident; show unread
awareness and the retained transcript. Explain what the cue does not establish:
attention, a promise to reply or private presence.

## Questions worth defending

- Why not infer availability? A seated or idle avatar can represent different
  intentions. A declaration gives another kind of information, with upkeep cost.
- Why not invitation-first? It may be preferable for strangers or strict DND.
  Our choice prioritizes quick familiar exchanges; that needs a pilot.
- What does Quiet reveal? Housemates still see name, coarse location and state.
  Public willingness and house preview suppression are coupled.
- What changed through AI work? Product-purpose research preceded the house code;
  review later made freshness and unread awareness visible. I challenged a
  repair-led crit argument and brought it back to the user goal and sacrificed
  capability.
- What has been checked? Code/native/browser observations and real live state
  delivery. Whether friends feel pressured or prefer Discord remains a user question.

Do not claim interviews, a successful human A/B, improved grades or original
invention of availability statuses. Explain the contextual choice and its cost.
