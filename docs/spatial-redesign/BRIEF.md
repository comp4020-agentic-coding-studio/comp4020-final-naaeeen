# Spatial redesign: make the house worth inhabiting

## Owner brief and current boundary

The owner rejects the current overall interaction design and invites substantial
changes. Research comparable games, record notes, question the product structure,
and compare purposeful alternatives before changing production behavior.

Their examples expose wider questions: the activity area has two physical seats
but can have five or six participants; using its activity should require being
seated there; the rest corner and central seating need distinct, practical
interactions; the bedroom's existing note/work-card mechanism is not useful to
them. These examples are not an exhaustive defect list. Existing layout, feature
choices, entry points and plans may be replaced when a better coherent design
justifies the change.

Retain the underlying requested experience: a warm, game-like place for two to six
known friends, controlled avatars, real text/presence, individually owned DIY rooms,
visits, shared study and an independently usable whiteboard. Published UI/docs stay
English; research can use any language. Work stays in the canonical WSL repository.
Local research/prototype commits are within the ongoing owner instruction; no new
remote release, provider, paid service or global permission change is included.

## The decision

What recurring thing should these friends come here to do, and how does each
place help them do it? Adding more activities is useful only if the physical
layout, participation rules and exit behavior make those activities understandable.
A higher test count cannot answer whether the activity is worth doing.

Every proposed zone must name its concrete user task, distinct useful effect,
capacity and visible roles, approach/activation, voluntary participation, departure,
late entry/reconnection, persistence/privacy, phone/accessibility route, and cost.
Ask what is lost if the same feature is just a global button. A weak answer means
the spatial role needs reconsideration; it is not a reason to hide an otherwise
useful accessible tool.

A bedroom answer must extend beyond decorating or filling in another form. A
visitor should not unexpectedly open their own global card at their friend's desk.
A chair should not silently enroll someone in a game or change Quiet/Can chat.
Seat eligibility and explicit activity consent must be specified together.
