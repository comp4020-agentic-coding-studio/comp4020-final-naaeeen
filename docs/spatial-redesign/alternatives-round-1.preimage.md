# Three different reasons to enter the house

These concepts were developed by a fresh-context Astra/max product researcher and
refined against parent-checked sources and the owner's constraints. They are design
proposals, not implemented capabilities or observed user preferences. The
[common scenario](COMPARISON-PROTOCOL.md) applies to all three. No numerical score
or preferred winner is supplied in this comparison packet.

## North: an after-class study night

**Recurring task:** familiar friends spend30–60minutes doing their actual work,
get a small amount of help when wanted, and take an optional shared break. The
app coordinates company and transitions; it does not replace their coursework tools.

- **Study table:** up to six deliberately join a common focus block with a visible
  ending. Joining and a later break are separate choices; ordinary seating remains
  possible. No required goal form, streak, public completion or proof-of-work claim.
  The independent board can explain a question when people choose to use it.
- **Activity table:** one capacity-matched cooperative tabletop for the whole group.
  Reuse the existing picture rules initially. Sit, then explicitly Join; fresh
  game actions stop on standing. The central/table geometry and visible roles must
  actually fit all residents, rather than showing two chairs and six participants.
- **Sofa/screen:** share one selected image or diagram, follow a common item/view,
  point something out and hand over the selection. It is a short show-and-chat
  break, distinct from editing a board or manipulating a puzzle. A video player
  is optional future work, not a promised shortcut.
- **Bedroom:** choose solo company or invite one or two people to look at a selected
  paragraph/error/reference with room-scoped conversation. The selected audience
  and common inspection context are the functional reason to visit. The visitor
  sees what the owner deliberately shares, not their own global task card.

**Six-person mixed intentions:** three may play, two keep working and one arrives
late. They do not trigger one another's interfaces. All six may later join the
activity; a small help room deliberately has a different audience/capacity.

**What is cut:** generic work-card entry from the primary HUD/desk, mandatory
check-ins, a new game engine, rewards, rich uploads and synchronized video in the
first slice. Preserve old cards and their inspection/export before hiding old entry
points. Decorative items can remain decorative; do not add fake chores to each.

**Pressure test:** a timer alone does not justify walking to a chair. A bedroom
that merely opens a note is also insufficient. This concept depends on useful
quiet/help/shared-break transitions. Private reference/admission changes and a
second shared inspection surface remain real work. If friends only use the timer
or ignore room visits, the spatial case is weak.

## East: a watch-and-play evening

**Recurring task:** friends spend30–45minutes catching up around content they choose
and one cooperative table activity. A quiet study spot remains for someone doing
something else, rather than controlling the entire evening.

- **Sofa/screen:** the primary shared session has a short queue, explicit viewers,
  current item/timeline, host/shared control and local volume. A participant can
  pause discussion or yield the remote; media never starts on a nonparticipant's
  screen. The normal player controls remain accessible.
- **Six-seat game table:** one existing cooperative puzzle, with everyone who wants
  a role able to contribute. A two-player game is not presented as the group option.
- **Study spot:** an independent quiet place, with no inherited playback/game state.
- **Bedroom:** a small invited visit around the owner's selected collection or
  current project. It needs a shared inspection task and an audience boundary;
  a pile of links or additional decoration is not enough.

**What is cut:** Four-in-a-row as the default, tournament queues, multiple new
games, a compulsory study routine and arbitrary embedded websites. The video
server coordinates state instead of carrying media bytes.

**Pressure test:** Discord already documents shared queuing and synchronized
controls; that is a credible competing workflow. YouTube supplies player methods,
not multiplayer synchronization, mobile autoplay recovery or our seat permissions.
gogh's documented Watch Party does not synchronize play/pause/seek. Our stronger
playback promise needs a real device/network spike. Bedroom utility is peripheral
to this loop and may remain weaker than a simple shared link.

## West: a small show-and-improve club

**Recurring task:** friends bring one work in progress, inspect it together,
compare directions and revisit an actual revision another day. Coming only to
observe must be normal; no participant is required to perform or prepare a piece.

- **Sofa/presentation screen:** follow one selected artifact and pointer, ask a
  question and pass presenting control. The visible presenter is not automatically
  the content's author.
- **Six-seat discussion table:** compare two or three versions, optionally collect
  independent reactions and reveal them together. The owner keeps the decision.
- **Study/board:** work alongside friends and sketch explanations with the existing
  independently accessible canvas.
- **Bedroom:** an owner-selected current/previous artifact display, with private
  review visits. Working files remain separate from explicitly shared material;
  external document access is still governed by its original service.

**What is cut:** generic work cards and competitive games from the primary flow;
start with one bounded artifact type, not every document format or live screen
sharing. A secondary cooperative game could be retained, but its cost must be added
rather than hidden inside the alternative.

**Pressure test:** this changes the main reason to enter from casual study/hangout
toward critique. Preparation and social evaluation can become work. It does not
answer the requested game area if games are removed outright. A gallery without
actual shared inspection repeats the decoration problem. The privacy/content
lifecycle is substantial despite a small-looking UI.

## Common discriminators

| Situation | North | East | West |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrive with nothing prepared | Use real coursework, observe or play | Watch/play immediately | Observe; creation contributes less material |
| Three take a break while two work | Separate study/game/viewing membership | Separate media audience and quiet place | Requires separate presentation and work membership |
| All six want active play | Existing cooperative table with six real positions | Existing cooperative table with six real positions | Game requirement is unresolved if both games are cut |
| Why enter an owned room? | A small selected-audience help/quiet session | An invited shared look at selected content | Inspect a revision in its curated context |
| Strongest competing shortcut | Existing study call/timer and group DM | Discord Watch Together | FigJam/design review and shared folder |
| Largest dependency | Helpful room audience/reference model | Reliable external playback on phones | Media authorization and preparation burden |

All three need honest seat/participation/exit semantics. A new game engine,
synchronized video, rich private uploads and a general room editor cannot be
quietly combined into a ten-day scope. The decision should select one primary
practice and a bounded implementation, not the union of attractive features.
